Ensembles & blends

GEFS – NOAA/NCEP Global Ensemble Forecast System, 31 members (control + 30), 0.25°. ECMWF IFS ENS – 51 members (the IFS control, which is the HRES run, plus 50 perturbed members), 0.25°. CMC GEPS – Canada's Global Ensemble Prediction System, 21 members (control + 20), published at 0.5°. NOAA AIGEFS – NOAA's AI-based Global Ensemble Forecast System (a GraphCast-derived model, 31 members: control + 30, 0.25°, 6-hourly). AIGEFS is started from GEFS initial conditions, so its members are not independent of GEFS, and it is published irregularly: when it is not yet available for a cycle the site is built without it and rebuilt once it arrives. All systems are shown on the same cycle and valid times.

Blend – choose Blend, then tick which systems to include (any two or more). The blended ensemble mean is the equal-weight average of the system means (each system counts once, whatever its size); the member minimum is the lowest value over every member of the chosen systems. L markers are the low centers of every member of the chosen systems, coloured by system (red GEFS, yellow ECMWF, teal GEPS, violet AIGEFS). In the minimum product each L is credited to the member that supplies the lowest value there. The cursor read-out lists each system's value and their difference (or, for three or more systems, the spread between the highest and lowest). GEPS is regridded from 0.5° to the finer grid when blended. Because AIGEFS shares GEFS's starting state, a GEFS + AIGEFS blend is not a blend of independent ensembles.

Swath – the accumulated member-wise minimum: at every point, the lowest pressure that any member of the chosen system(s) forecasts at any hour from F000 up to the hour shown. It builds up like a storm-track swath as you step or play through the forecast, and never rises. The L markers are the deepest points of the swath; the cursor read-out says at which forecast hour (and valid time) the minimum was reached and which member supplies it.

Probability – the share of members (or, in a blend, the equal-weight average of the systems' shares) whose pressure is below the chosen level at the hour shown. The levels are the storm-intensity thresholds of the colour scale (1005 gale … 925 hPa Cat 5) plus 1010. Hover for the count; in a blend the read-out lists each system.

Wind and gust – the Field switch (P W U) turns the same products into 10 m wind speed and wind gust in mph, for coastal-storm and Nor’easter work: the ensemble mean; the highest member (the worst case in any member; the cursor read-out names the member); the swath (the highest value any member reaches at any hour up to the one shown, with the hour it happens); and the probability that wind or gusts reach a chosen threshold (wind: 25, 31, 39, 50, 55, 74 mph; gusts: 35, 46, 58, 74, 90 mph, which include the NWS Wind Advisory criteria of 31 mph sustained and 46 mph gusts, and the 58 mph High Wind Warning gust criterion). Wind comes from all four systems; gusts are forecast by GEFS and ECMWF only. GEPS and AIGEFS show an estimated gust (marked “est.”): each member’s wind speed times a gust factor that depends on the wind speed and on land versus water, fitted to what GEFS and ECMWF forecast. It is a statistical estimate, not a model forecast; over water it falls between the two models, over land it tends to understate the strongest gusts. Switching to Gust keeps the systems with a real gust forecast; add the estimated ones with the blend tick-boxes. The mean of gusts is smoother and lower than the strongest gusts any member shows, so use the highest-member, swath and probability products for the damaging-wind risk. ECMWF’s gust is the maximum over the preceding 3 hours (1 hour on the early steps; none at F000), whereas GEFS’s is an instantaneous value, so the two are not exactly alike. These are model 10 m winds on a 0.25° grid: they smooth out terrain, shorelines and local effects, and ensembles of this size under-sample the extremes.

Member low tracks and strike probability – every member's low centers are linked from hour to hour into tracks (switch them on with K; the part up to the hour shown is solid, the rest faint). Strike counts, at each point, the share of members that had a low deeper than the chosen level pass within the chosen radius at any hour from F000 up to the hour shown (like the swath it accumulates). A low in one member is counted once however long it lingers. Tracks are short-lived and only follow lows that the detector finds (below 1005 hPa), so read the percentages as indicative.

Animated GIF – File ▸ Export animated GIF (or the GIF button) renders the current map view, product, ensemble and options for a range of forecast hours entirely in your browser and downloads a compressed GIF: one shared palette, and after the first frame only the pixels that change are stored.

Colour scale – the colour changes fall on storm-intensity thresholds: Gale (34–47 kt) ≈ 1000–1004 hPa, Storm (48–63 kt) ≈ 990–999, then hurricane categories Cat 1 ≈ 975–989, Cat 2 965–974, Cat 3 950–964, Cat 4 925–949 and Cat 5 below 925 hPa. Wind thresholds come from the NWS marine warnings and the Saffir-Simpson wind scale, converted to pressure with NOAA’s Dvorak table (rounded to 5 hPa). It is a rough guide only: pressure does not determine wind speed, so an extratropical low can be gale or storm force at higher pressure, and a low in a band is not a hurricane.

All data are stored as compressed per-system grids; the blends, the swath, the colouring and the L markers of the minimum products are computed in your browser, so any combination of systems can be blended without extra downloads of pre-built maps.

ECMWF's 06/18Z runs only reach 6 days, so on such a cycle the ECMWF and blend views end at F144.
